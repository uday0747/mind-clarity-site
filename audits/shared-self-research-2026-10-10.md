## Sources read

Read the Sanskrit, translation, and commentary in Swami Lokeswarananda’s Chandogya edition for [6.1.4](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/chandogya-upanishad-english/d/doc239255.html), [6.2.1](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/chandogya-upanishad-english/d/doc239260.html), [6.3.2](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/chandogya-upanishad-english/d/doc239266.html), and [6.8.7](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/chandogya-upanishad-english/d/doc239302.html).

The sequence combines the clay analogy (objects share an underlying substance), existence before manifestation, entry as living self, and identity with the subtle Self of all. The website explicitly attributes the non-dual reading to this commentator; it does not imply all Vedanta schools agree on absolute identity. The clause about emergence from non-existence in 6.2.1 is a reported opposing view, not the teaching being endorsed. Modern commentary remarks about other religions or science are not adopted as independent facts. The analogy explains a philosophical argument; it does not experimentally demonstrate that individuals planned every event before birth.

Read [Sri Aurobindo’s Isha Upanishad, Complete Works volume 17](https://www.sriaurobindoashram.org/sriaurobindo/downloadpdf.php?id=32), official Ashram edition ©2003. The publisher distinguishes the final translation/commentary in Part One from unfinished manuscripts in Part Two. Verses 6–7 appear on printed page 7 / PDF page 17. They link perception of oneness with freedom from aversion, delusion, and grief. Their relevance to relationships is explained positively on the website. These are realization teachings, not a clinical treatment study or guaranteed financial outcome. Only these selected passages and publication notes were reviewed; no claim of reading all 607 PDF pages.
