### Does Ayurveda explain health solely through natal planets?

No such conclusion follows from these passages. [Charaka Samhita, Sutra Sthana 11.41–45](https://www.siva.sh/caraka-samhita/sutra-sthana/11/41-45) includes Sanskrit and English. Verse 11.42 explains time through seasonal patterns and their abnormal expression; 11.43 lists unsuitable sensory contact, errors of judgment, and time/seasonal effects; 11.45 distinguishes endogenous, exogenous, and psychological disease categories.

The professional [Charaka Samhita Online chapter](https://www.carakasamhitaonline.com/index.php?title=Tistraishaniya_Adhyaya) corroborates the framework. It provides translator Tiwari P., reviewer/editor names, and DOI 10.47468/CSNE.2020.e01.s01.013. Its search-indexed text was available; direct retrieval timed out. The site links directly readable verses. These are traditional classifications, not a replacement for contemporary diagnosis.

### Are disease associations present in classical astrology?

Yes. [Brihat Jataka 23.7](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/brihat-jataka-by-varahamihira-sanskrit-english/d/doc1501868.html) provides Sanskrit and Michael D. Neely’s 2007 translation. It connects planetary configurations to forms of illness. This verifies that the tradition contains these claims. It does not measure accuracy, demonstrate planetary causation, or validate disease-onset forecasts. The website gives a historical explanation rather than a disease-prediction checklist. Historical disease labels and modern diagnostic equivalents also require care.

### Is there empirical research about birth timing and health?

[Boland et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26041386/) analyzed 1,749,400 individuals and 1,688 conditions from one medical system, finding 55 disease categories associated with birth month after statistical correction. The authors discuss seasonal early-development mechanisms. This is retrospective population evidence about birth month, not a natal planetary experiment or an individual forecast.

[Borsi, published 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28269826/), examined three geographically distinct cohorts totaling 11.8 million individuals. It replicated four of nine new cardiovascular associations from the earlier study; the new non-cardiovascular findings did not emerge as significant. It also found new associations, few reproduced across cohorts. Results varied with geography and cohort composition. This qualification is included on the site so the earlier result is not presented as universally predictive.

### Do personal testimonies prove the complete framework?

Testimonies can record what someone experienced or believed. They require identifiable authors and faithful context. They do not independently settle cosmology, planetary causation, diagnosis, or guaranteed outcomes. Existing Ramana, Vivekananda, and first-person meditation sources remain useful with these distinctions. This batch adds no unverifiable miracle testimonials.

## On-demand continuation: creation as play

Examined Brahma Sutra 2.1.33 in George Thibaut’s translations of [Shankara’s commentary, 1890](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/brahma-sutras-thibaut/d/doc63841.html) and [Ramanuja’s commentary, 1904](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/brahma-sutras-ramanuja/d/doc1083849.html). Both explain creation through play, providing a direct classical connection with the game analogy. Shankara connects activity with divine nature and the teaching that Brahman is the Self of everything; Ramanuja discusses creation, sustenance, and destruction by divine will. Their distinct systems should not be merged into a single doctrine. Both describe fulfillment, so Uday’s loneliness/need-for-experience explanation remains his further formulation. No exact life-event schedule is established by this verse.
