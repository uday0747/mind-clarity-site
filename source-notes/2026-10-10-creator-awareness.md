## Uday Kumar philosophy and teaching direction
Uday’s central teaching is that we are the shared Creator who made and entered the game of experiences. Knowing who we are is intended to change our perspective and confidence, rather than relying only on instructions to behave well. Recognition does not end enjoyment: in his account, both knowing and not knowing occur within the game, and recognition allows experiences to be approached with greater peace.

Uday asks learners to examine the complexity of the body and nature as invitations to enquire into the source of creation. He discusses meditation, comfortable upright sitting, breathing and the felt experience of energy. These explanations are his contemplative interpretations; physiological details require separate checking before publication.

Uday wants earlier contemplative traditions studied respectfully, including their discoveries and mistakes. He requests a source-based comparison of Ramana Maharshi, Swami Vivekananda, Sri Ramakrishna, Yogi Vemana, Ashtavakra and other teachers associated with enlightenment. Exact passages, translations and differences between traditions should be retained rather than treating all teachers as making identical claims.

His criticism concerns fear, exploitation, commercial interests, superiority and conflict conducted in the name of religion. His proposed teaching is not to establish another religion, but to help people recognise shared divine identity and think independently. Bhakti is understood as a path towards that recognition.

At the role level, people continue working, learning and taking precautions. The sense of agency, effort and changing understanding are also parts of the unfolding game. Do not turn the shared Creator teaching into a claim that an individual personality can rewrite the total design.

Uday emphasises allowing people a chance to change rather than responding to wrongdoing with killing. He discusses protecting innocent people, careful checks, practical precautions and bringing knowledge into society.
